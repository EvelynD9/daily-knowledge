Every map makes an argument

A map is a selective model of the world, not a neutral copy of it.

The idea

Mapmakers choose projection, scale, boundaries, labels, and what to exclude. Those choices affect which places appear central, large, connected, or important.

An example

The Mercator projection preserves direction for navigation but greatly enlarges landmasses near the poles.

Put it into practice

Before trusting a map, ask what purpose it was designed to serve and what its design distorts.
