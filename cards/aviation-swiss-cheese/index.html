Safety depends on layers, not perfection

The Swiss-cheese model explains accidents as the alignment of weaknesses across several protective layers.

The idea

Each defence—training, procedures, technology, supervision—has gaps. An incident can occur when active errors and hidden system weaknesses line up at the same moment.

An example

A checklist may catch a setup error, but if time pressure, unclear roles, and a missed cross-check align, several defences can fail together.

Put it into practice

After an error, look beyond the final person involved and ask which earlier barriers could have prevented it.
