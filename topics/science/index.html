Good beliefs update with evidence

Bayesian thinking means adjusting confidence when new evidence arrives—not switching between absolute certainty and doubt.

Correlation is a clue, not a cause

Two things can move together without one producing the other.

A useful claim must risk being wrong

A scientific claim should make predictions that evidence could, in principle, contradict.

Heat spreads unless work is done

Entropy describes how energy spreads and how many microscopic arrangements can produce the same overall state.

Human intuition is poor at huge scales

We understand differences like ten versus twenty, but struggle to feel the gap between a million and a billion.

Extreme results often move toward average

Regression to the mean occurs when an unusually high or low measurement is followed by one closer to typical.

Sleep is part of learning

Learning does not end when practice stops; sleep helps stabilise and reorganise new memories.

Simple rules can create complex behaviour

Emergence occurs when interactions among simple parts produce a pattern that no single part controls.
